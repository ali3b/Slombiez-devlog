# v0.13: echoes, groups, and an inner voice

*1 November 2026 · v0.13.0-alpha*

## Whispering to the dead
Every zombie in Slombiez was someone in this town, and what's left of them still drifts toward the house they lived in, the place they worked, and the people they loved. Now you can stir that. Inspect a walking corpse and press **D-pad ▼**:
- *"Go home."*
- *"Lucy. Find Lucy."* (only people they loved, alive or risen)
- *"The church bells. Remember."* (places that exist in this town)

If it lands, they turn toward it, and the inspector reads *"Following something you whispered."* If not: *"Nothing. They're too far gone."* Each pull wears off a little. **Honest result from the test:** two of three whispered-home dead moved toward home, then all three were dragged off by a noise within six minutes. Hunger beats memory, which is the right priority.

## Safety in numbers
The runner showed survivors in a falling town hiding in separate houses all day: only about 3% of them out and about in daylight. A day alone in a house, people think about the family holding out across the street. Now **survivors who are alone make a daylight dash to join a bigger group** when the way looks clear: the lonely, the sociable, the ones with someone they care about over there. It's the most dangerous minute of their day.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Daytime life in falling towns | 3% | **6%** |
| Variety score | 78 | **82** |
| Survivors at day 3 (all towns) | 29.5% | 26.9% (the dash is dangerous) |
| Phase 2 gate checks | 5/5 pass | 5/5 pass |

It's a modest gain, not a fix. At any moment only 0.2–0.5 people per falling town are crossing. Most survivors are already in groups, or the dead are at their door.

## Hybrid minds (optional)
Your original brief asked for thoughts that could use OpenRouter, a local OpenAI-compatible server, or Anthropic. That's in now, and **completely optional**: without it the game runs exactly as before, offline.

Put `llm.secrets.json` next to the game (the format is in README.txt). Two things light up:
- **Typed whispers are understood more deeply.** The model only *translates* your words into the same closed set of effects (goals, beliefs, fears, urges, moods, trust). People come only from that person's own life, and places only from this town. Every effect is validated: a made-up "teleport" urge or a stranger's name is simply dropped. If the model refuses, fails or isn't there, the offline parser takes over.
- **An inner voice.** Whoever you inspect gets one fresh sentence of monologue every half minute, written only from what *they* know. Someone who hasn't seen the dead yet is still thinking about their ordinary day.

The key file never goes into the repository, and release packages are built without it.
